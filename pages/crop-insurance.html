Crop Insurance: Your Safety Net

"Insure the crap out of the commodities!" - Farm Con 2026 Recap

Why Crop Insurance is CRITICAL Right Now

"We are NOT the low cost producer" + "Soybeans possible sub $10" = Crop insurance isn't optional, it's survival.

The Big Beautiful Bill has strengthened crop insurance programs significantly. This is the time to maximize your coverage.

What the Big Beautiful Bill Did for Crop Insurance

Increased Premium Subsidies

Higher government cost-share on premiums makes comprehensive coverage more affordable.

  • Subsidies up to 80% on some policies
  • Enhanced support for higher coverage levels
  • Beginning farmer additional subsidies

Higher Coverage Options

Access to higher coverage levels with better subsidy support.

  • Coverage up to 85% available
  • Enterprise unit discounts enhanced
  • Improved prevented planting provisions

Program Improvements

Better alignment with actual farm risk and production.

  • Updated T-yields and APH calculations
  • Enhanced quality loss provisions
  • Streamlined claims process

New Options

Additional tools to layer protection.

  • Enhanced Coverage Option (ECO)
  • Supplemental Coverage Option (SCO)
  • Stacked Income Protection (STAX)

Types of Crop Insurance

Revenue Protection (RP) - RECOMMENDED

The gold standard for most operations. Protects against both yield loss AND price decline.

  • What it covers: Revenue loss from yield reduction, price decline, or combination of both
  • Price protection: Uses higher of projected price or harvest price - protects if prices DROP
  • Coverage levels: 50% to 85% of expected revenue
  • Best for: Most grain operations, especially with sub-$10 bean risk

Yield Protection (YP)

Protects against yield loss only. Uses projected price (no harvest price option).

  • What it covers: Yield shortfall only - doesn't protect against price drops
  • Lower premium: Cheaper than RP but less protection
  • Best for: Operations with strong forward contracts locked in

RP-HPE (Revenue Protection - Harvest Price Exclusion)

Like RP but excludes harvest price increase benefit. Lower premium option.

  • What it covers: Revenue loss, but guarantee won't increase if harvest price rises
  • Consideration: Costs less but misses upside if prices spike

CLIP - Catastrophic Risk Protection

CAT (Catastrophic) coverage - also known as CLIP - is the basic, low-cost entry point to crop insurance.

What is CLIP/CAT?

  • Coverage: 50% of approved yield at 55% of price
  • Cost: $655 administrative fee (no premium)
  • Pays when: Catastrophic loss occurs
  • Required for: Some FSA program eligibility

How to Purchase CLIP

  1. Contact FSA Office - CAT can be purchased through FSA
  2. OR Crop Insurance Agent - Any authorized agent
  3. Deadline: Sales closing date (March 15 for spring crops in CO)
  4. Fee: $655 per crop per county

CLIP vs Buy-Up Coverage

Important: CLIP is MINIMUM coverage. Given the market outlook, consider buying up to higher coverage levels.

Recommendation: With sub-$10 beans possible, CLIP alone may not be enough. Talk to your agent about 75-85% RP coverage.

Unit Structure Options

Enterprise Units

All acres of a crop in county = one unit

  • Lower premium (significant discount)
  • Losses averaged across all acres
  • Best when: Consistent production across farms

Optional Units

Separate unit by section or FSN

  • Higher premium
  • Each unit stands alone for losses
  • Best when: Variable production, localized risks

Basic Units

By ownership share

  • Middle ground option
  • Separate units for owned vs rented

Layering Additional Coverage

SCO - Supplemental Coverage Option

County-level coverage that fills gap between your coverage and 86%.

  • Coverage: From your elected level up to 86%
  • Trigger: County-level losses
  • Note: Cannot use with ARC-CO

ECO - Enhanced Coverage Option

Additional area-based coverage at highest levels (86-95%).

  • Coverage: 90% or 95% trigger
  • Trigger: Area revenue or yield
  • Can use with: Any underlying policy

Action Items for Our Operation

  • BEFORE March 15: Meet with crop insurance agent
  • Evaluate: Current coverage level - is 70-75% enough given sub-$10 bean risk?
  • Consider: Moving to 80% or 85% RP coverage
  • Review: Unit structure - enterprise vs optional units
  • Ask about: SCO and ECO to layer additional protection
  • Calculate: Break-even and what coverage level protects it

Questions to Ask Your Agent

  • What's the premium difference between 75% and 80% RP?
  • How does enterprise vs optional unit affect my premium and coverage?
  • Should I add SCO or ECO given current market outlook?
  • What's my projected price and how does that affect my guarantee?
  • What documentation do I need for prevented planting claims?
  • How do quality loss provisions work?
  • What's the best strategy given sub-$10 bean projections?
