Carbon Markets: Be Careful

Important warnings and considerations before selling carbon credits

The Warning from FARM Con

"Be careful on selling carbon."

The experts are telling us to proceed with caution. Here's why and what to consider.

Why the Caution?

Long-Term Lockups

Many carbon contracts lock you into practices for 10, 20, even 100 years. What happens when:

  • You want to sell the farm?
  • A new, better opportunity comes along?
  • Your farming situation changes?
  • The next generation has different plans?
Risk: You might be giving away future flexibility for small payments today.

Undervalued Today

Current carbon credit prices may significantly undervalue what these practices will be worth in the future.

  • Carbon prices are expected to rise significantly
  • Early sellers may lock in low rates
  • Demand for carbon credits is growing
  • Regulations are tightening globally
Risk: Selling now at $15-20/ton when future prices could be $50-100+/ton.

Measurement Uncertainty

How carbon is measured and verified is still evolving. Contracts based on today's methods may have issues.

  • Different programs measure differently
  • Verification standards changing
  • Your credits might not be recognized later
  • Additionality requirements vary
Risk: You might not get credit for what you're actually sequestering.

Contract Complexity

Carbon contracts are complex legal documents with terms that favor buyers.

  • Reversal clauses (if carbon is released)
  • Monitoring requirements
  • Penalty provisions
  • Transferability restrictions
Risk: Fine print can create unexpected liabilities.

The Real Opportunity: Traceability

Better Strategy

Instead of selling carbon credits, focus on building traceability systems that document your practices. This:

  • Keeps YOUR options open
  • Positions you for FUTURE carbon opportunities at better prices
  • Unlocks OTHER premiums (sustainability, low-carbon, etc.)
  • Gives you data to negotiate from strength

If You're Considering Carbon Sales

✅ Questions to Ask BEFORE Signing

  • Contract Length How long are you committed? What are the exit terms?
  • Price Structure Is the price locked? Can it adjust with market? What's the floor?
  • Practice Requirements Exactly what practices are required? Can they change?
  • Reversal Liability What happens if carbon is released? Natural disaster? Change in practice?
  • Measurement Method How is carbon measured? Who pays for verification? How often?
  • Stacking Rights Can you participate in other programs? Government programs? Other certifications?
  • Transfer with Sale What happens if you sell the farm? Does contract transfer?
  • Data Ownership Who owns the data you provide? How can they use it?

Alternative Approaches

Wait and Document

Implement the practices, document everything, but DON'T sell the credits yet. You'll have options later when prices are higher.

Lower Risk

Short-Term Pilots

If you want to test carbon markets, look for shorter-term pilot programs (1-3 years) to learn the system without long commitments.

Learning

Insets vs Credits

Some companies want "insets" (direct relationships with farms) rather than tradeable credits. These may have better terms.

Alternative

Government Programs First

EQIP, CSP, and other programs pay for similar practices WITHOUT locking up your carbon. Do these first.

Questions for Further Research

What We Need to Learn

  • Which carbon programs have the fairest terms?
  • What's the range of prices being offered locally?
  • Are any farmers we know in carbon contracts? What's their experience?
  • What do our lenders/landlords think about carbon contracts?
  • How do carbon sales interact with conservation programs (CSP, EQIP)?
  • What's the legal advice - have an ag attorney review?
