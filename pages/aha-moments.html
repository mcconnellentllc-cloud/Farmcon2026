The Consolidation Reality: Fewer Farms, Bigger Stakes

American farming has transformed dramatically. The farms that remain are larger, more productive - and the pressure has never been higher.

Year # of Farms Avg Farm Size % of US on Farms
1900 5.7 million 147 acres 40%
1950 5.4 million 205 acres ~15%
1992 1.9 million 491 acres ~2%
2017 2.04 million 441 acres ~1%
2024 1.88 million 466 acres ~1%

What We've Lost Since 1950

  • 3.5 million farms - 66% gone
  • 323 million acres - size of Texas x2
  • 140,000 farms lost just 2017-2022

What Remains is Stronger

  • Farm size 3x larger than 1950
  • 40% more production value (inflation-adjusted: 17%)
  • Technology + efficiency = fewer people, more output

The path to consolidation continues. Those who remain must be smarter, more efficient, better documented. That's why traceability, AI, and risk management matter more than ever.

Source: USDA ERS, American Farm Bureau

Competing for Land: Farmers vs. Institutional Buyers

Bill Gates owns 270,000 acres. Pension funds and private equity are pouring billions into farmland. How do you compete?

The Challenge

  • 40% of US farmland now owned by non-farmers
  • $12+ billion spent by institutions in 2021 alone
  • Institutional ownership up 10x in 15 years (now 2% of all farmland)
  • Farmland prices up 400% - massive appreciation
  • 18 million acres projected out of farming by 2035
  • 80% of farmland owned by someone 55+ (400M acres changing hands soon)

The Good News

  • 60% of sales still go to farmers, not investors
  • Only 2% of sales go to big institutions
  • "Farmers always outbid us" - Farmland Partners CEO
  • Emotional connection beats spreadsheets at auction

How Farmers Stay Competitive

  1. Build relationships with neighbors NOW - Be the first call when land comes available. Most sales never hit the open market.
  2. Keep financials strong - Low debt ratios, strong cash flow, established lender relationships let you move fast.
  3. Use every advantage - FSA loans, beginning farmer programs, seller financing - options institutions can't access.
  4. Think multi-generational - Institutions need 8-10% returns. Family farms can think in decades, not quarters.
  5. Document everything - Your yield data, soil health, and operational knowledge make YOU the best buyer for adjacent land.
  6. Consider creative structures - Lease-to-own, right of first refusal, gradual buyouts from retiring neighbors.

Watch for the Wave

400 million acres will change hands as the 55+ generation transitions. Position yourself NOW:

  • Talk to neighbors about their plans
  • Build relationship with local ag lenders
  • Explore FSA loan programs for land purchases
  • Consider forming partnerships with family members to pool resources

Sources: Halderman Real Estate, National Farmers Union

"Hold and Wait" - The Hundred Bagger

US farmland values indexed to 1910 show a hockey stick since 2000. The chart doesn't lie.

The Pattern (USDA/Census Data 1910-2023)

  • 1910-1980: Relatively flat, modest gains
  • 1980s: Farm crisis dip
  • 1990-2000: Gradual recovery
  • 2000-2023: Exponential growth - the hockey stick
  • Result: ~1,400% increase from 1910 baseline

Why Farmland Wins Long-Term

  • They're not making more of it
  • Population keeps growing
  • Food demand only increases
  • Inflation hedge - hard asset
  • Low correlation to stocks/bonds

The "100 Baggers" Mindset

  • Think in generations, not years
  • Institutions need quick returns - you don't
  • Time is your competitive advantage
  • Every acre you hold appreciates
  • Your grandkids will thank you

The Family Farm Advantage

Bill Gates and pension funds need 8-10% annual returns to justify their investment. You don't.

You can:

  • Hold through down cycles (they can't)
  • Think in 30-year horizons (they think in quarters)
  • Accept modest cash returns for massive appreciation
  • Build legacy value that compounds across generations

The lesson: Don't sell. Hold and wait.

The Lakers vs. S&P 500 Lesson

Peter Mallouk (Creative Planning) comparison:

  • LA Lakers: $67.5M (1979) → $10 billion today
  • S&P 500: $67.5M (1979) → $13.7 billion today

The "boring" index beats the trophy asset by $3.7 billion.

The point: Consistent, patient investing beats flashy bets. Same applies to farmland - hold, let it compound, don't chase shiny objects.

Be an OWNER, Not Just an Operator

"The ability to stay ahead of inflation... be an owner... make the business work... be a business owner."

Operator vs. Owner Mindset

Operator Owner
Trades time for money Builds equity that compounds
Rents land Owns land (appreciates)
Sells commodity Owns the value chain
Works IN the business Works ON the business
Income stops when you stop Assets work while you sleep

Ownership Beats Inflation

  • Cash loses value - inflation erodes purchasing power
  • Wages chase inflation - always behind
  • Owners win: Land, equipment, inventory, brands appreciate
  • Debt gets cheaper - fixed payments, inflating dollars
  • Commodity prices rise - eventually

Inflation punishes savers and rewards owners.

The Big Risk: "When the world decides to stop loaning money to the US..."

The US runs on borrowed money. What happens when that stops?

  • Dollar devalues - your cash buys less
  • Interest rates spike - borrowing becomes expensive
  • Inflation explodes - everything costs more
  • Hard assets win - land, commodities, real things hold value

Farmland is the ultimate hedge: You own something real. You produce something essential. People always need to eat.

When paper wealth gets questioned, dirt doesn't.

The counterpoint: "The US is the cleanest shirt in the dirty laundry."

Yes, the US has problems. But Europe, China, Japan - they all have bigger ones. Capital still flows here because we're the least bad option. That buys time, but it's not a permanent solution.

Plan for both scenarios: the US stays strong AND prepare if it doesn't. Either way, owning productive land wins.

Make the Business Work

Thinking like an owner means:

  • Know your numbers - cost per bushel, cost per head, break-even points
  • Build systems - the business should run without you in the tractor
  • Acquire assets - every year, own a little more
  • Create value - differentiation, brands, direct relationships
  • Think generationally - you're building something to pass on

You're not just a farmer. You're a business owner who happens to farm.

"Short-run money is to lend. Long-term money is to own."

Time Horizon Strategy Examples
Short-term
(1-3 years)		 LEND Savings, CDs, T-bills, operating reserves
Long-term
(10+ years)		 OWN Land, equipment, businesses, stocks, your farm

Your operating account? Keep it liquid - lend it. Your generational wealth? Own things that appreciate and produce.
