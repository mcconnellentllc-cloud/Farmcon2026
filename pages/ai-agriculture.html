Insights from Zack Kass - AI Futurist, Former Head of Go-to-Market at OpenAI

"Machine intelligence is getting much cheaper."

The cost of AI has collapsed dramatically - making these tools accessible for operations of ANY size:

What this means for farmers: The most powerful AI tools that were only affordable for big corporations 2-3 years ago are now accessible to everyone. A task that cost $60 in 2023 now costs $1.30.

What Are AI Tokens?

A token is roughly a piece of a word - think of it as how AI "reads" and "writes" text.

Token Basics

  • 1 token = approximately 4 characters or 3/4 of a word
  • 1,000 tokens = approximately 750 words
  • 1 million tokens = approximately 750,000 words (several novels worth)

Real World Examples

  • A text message: ~20-50 tokens
  • A paragraph: ~100-200 tokens
  • A full page of text: ~500-700 tokens
  • A detailed crop report: ~1,000-2,000 tokens

What This Means in Practice

At today's prices ($0.10 - $1.30 per million tokens):

  • Analyze a full season of field notes = pennies
  • Generate crop insurance documentation = a few cents
  • Research market conditions = less than a dollar
  • Draft a marketing plan = cents, not dollars

Bottom line: Cost is no longer a barrier. These tools are essentially free compared to just 2-3 years ago.

Key Concept: Agentic AI

The shift from "assistants" to "agents"

Zack discussed the evolution of AI from simple chatbots to agentic AI - AI systems that can:

  • Take actions - not just answer questions, but actually DO things
  • Complete multi-step tasks - break down complex goals into steps and execute them
  • Use tools - interact with other software, databases, and systems
  • Work autonomously - operate with minimal human oversight once given a goal

What This Means for Farming:

Instead of asking AI "What's the weather forecast?" you could tell an AI agent:

  • "Monitor weather and soil moisture, and alert me when conditions are right for planting"
  • "Track commodity prices and notify me when my target sell price is hit"
  • "Compile my field records into the format my grain buyer needs for their sustainability program"
  • "Research and summarize new USDA programs I might qualify for"

The AI handles the execution, not just the information.

The Automation Boundary

Zack gave simple examples of where AI is heading:

  • "Tell my AI to fill my fridge" - AI handles routine grocery decisions
  • "Fill my date night" - AI plans based on your preferences

The key question: Where's YOUR automation boundary?

For farming, think about:

  • Automate: Weather monitoring, price alerts, report generation, routine ordering
  • AI-assisted: Marketing timing, input decisions, equipment purchases
  • Human decision: Major contracts, land decisions, family/succession planning

The automation boundary moves as you build trust with the tools and as the tools get better.

Key Insight: Personalization at Scale

"We as people have a ton of preferences"

Every farmer, every operation, every field is different. AI can adapt to YOUR specific:

Your Operation

  • Your specific crops and rotation
  • Your soil types and conditions
  • Your equipment and capabilities
  • Your risk tolerance

Your Preferences

  • How you want information delivered
  • What level of detail you need
  • When and how often to be alerted
  • Your communication style

The point: AI isn't one-size-fits-all. It learns YOUR preferences and adapts to how YOU work. Unlike generic software, AI can be trained on your specific situation.

"We make about 125 purchase decisions a day"

Think about all the decisions you make running a farm operation - inputs, timing, marketing, equipment, repairs, hiring... the mental load is enormous.

AI can help by:

  • Filtering information so you see what matters
  • Analyzing options and presenting recommendations
  • Handling routine decisions automatically based on your rules
  • Freeing up your mental bandwidth for the decisions that really need your judgment

Implementing AI on the Farm

When AI makes purchase decisions - how do you actually implement this?

Start With Rules You Already Follow

You already have decision rules in your head. Write them down:

  • "When fertilizer price hits X, buy Y tons"
  • "Reorder parts when inventory drops below Z"
  • "If basis improves by $0.10, sell another 10%"
  • "When GDD reaches target, schedule scout"

Layer the Automation

  1. Level 1: AI monitors and alerts you
  2. Level 2: AI recommends, you approve
  3. Level 3: AI executes within limits you set
  4. Level 4: Full automation for routine decisions

Move up levels as you build trust

Practical Farm Examples

  • Input Purchasing Set price targets, quantity limits, and timing windows. AI watches markets and executes or alerts.
  • Grain Marketing Define your marketing plan rules. AI monitors basis, futures, and triggers sales per your strategy.
  • Parts & Supplies Auto-reorder consumables. AI tracks usage patterns and maintains inventory levels.
  • Scouting & Timing Weather and GDD monitoring. AI schedules activities based on conditions you define.

"We are approaching unmetered intelligence"

A profound insight from Zack on where AI is headed.

What This Means

Intelligence - the ability to analyze, reason, and solve problems - is becoming essentially unlimited and nearly free.

  • No more rationing: You won't have to decide "is this problem worth paying to analyze?"
  • Unlimited questions: Ask AI anything, anytime, without worrying about cost
  • Always-on analysis: Continuous monitoring and optimization becomes feasible
  • Democratized expertise: Small operations get same analytical power as large corporations

For Farmers

Think about what you would do if expert analysis cost NOTHING:

  • Real-time market analysis on every sale decision
  • Soil and crop analysis without expensive consultants
  • Legal and contract review before every agreement
  • Custom agronomic recommendations for every field
  • 24/7 monitoring of weather, markets, equipment

That future is arriving faster than most realize.

"How and who chooses to use it will define brilliance"

The critical follow-up to unmetered intelligence.

The New Competitive Advantage

When everyone has access to unlimited intelligence, the differentiator becomes:

  • WHO actually uses it - Most won't. Those who do gain advantage.
  • HOW they apply it - Using AI for the right problems, in the right ways
  • WHEN they start - Early adopters compound their learning advantage
  • Willingness to change - Adapting workflows and processes

For Farmers: This is Your Edge

The advantage won't go to the biggest operations or the most tech-savvy. It will go to those who:

  • Actually try it - Start using AI tools today, even imperfectly
  • Ask better questions - Learn what to ask and how
  • Apply it to real problems - Not theoretical, but your actual decisions
  • Iterate and improve - Get a little better each time

The farmer who starts TODAY has an advantage over the one who waits.

The "Super Genius" Path

The middle ground is disappearing. Those who embrace AI as a tool for growth will pull ahead. Those who coast will fall behind. The choice is yours.

  • For the next generation: Teach them to USE AI, not be replaced by it
  • For yourself: Stay curious, keep learning, leverage the tools
  • For your operation: The farms that adopt and adapt will outcompete those that don't

Four Big Risks to Watch

1. The Idiocracy Warning

"An individual may discover critical thinking is no longer relevant"

If AI does all our thinking, we might lose the ability to think critically ourselves. Use it or lose it.

2. Dehumanization

The risk of losing what makes us human

When AI handles all interactions, decisions, and creativity - what role is left for human connection, judgment, and meaning?

3. Bad Actors

"Psychos and financial criminals"

AI amplifies capability for everyone - including those with bad intentions. Scams, fraud, and manipulation become more sophisticated and harder to detect.

4. Job Displacement

What happens to work and workers?

As AI handles more tasks, entire job categories may disappear. The transition will be uneven - some thrive, many struggle.

The Balance

  • Use AI as a tool, not a replacement for judgment and connection
  • Stay engaged: Understand WHY, not just WHAT
  • Preserve human relationships: In farming, trust and handshakes still matter
  • Keep learning: Don't let skills atrophy - stay sharp
  • Model for the next generation: Show them human values alongside AI tools

The goal: AI should amplify human intelligence, not replace it.

"We want it cheaper, better, faster."

The Case FOR Embracing AI

  • Cheaper: Lower costs for analysis, advice, monitoring - accessible to all farm sizes
  • Better: More accurate predictions, fewer errors, better decisions
  • Faster: Instant answers, real-time monitoring, rapid response

Every generation of farmers has adopted new technology - tractors, GPS, precision ag. AI is the next step.

The Farmer's Opportunity

  • AI can make your operation more competitive
  • Small farms can access analysis that only big operations could afford
  • Time saved on paperwork = more time for farming
  • Better decisions = better margins

The risks are real, but so is the opportunity. The key is to engage thoughtfully.

The Ultimate Question: "How will it reduce suffering and increase joy?"

Zack's framing for evaluating any AI application.

The True Measure of AI

Not "is it profitable?" or "is it efficient?" - but does it actually make human life better?

  • Reduce suffering: Less drudgery, less stress, less uncertainty, less isolation
  • Increase joy: More time for what matters, more connection, more meaning

For Farming

  • Reduce suffering: Less paperwork stress, less guesswork on decisions, less sleepless nights about markets
  • Increase joy: More time with family, more time in the field (not the office), more confidence in decisions

Every AI tool you consider: Does it pass this test?

"Expand Human Potential"

"The amount we can do today is astronomical vs the 1800s."

The Historical Perspective

Every generation has expanded what's possible:

  • 1800s: A farmer could work one field with hand tools and animals
  • 1900s: Tractors and mechanization multiplied output 10x
  • 2000s: GPS, precision ag, and data let one person manage thousands of acres
  • 2020s+: AI adds unlimited analytical capacity to all of the above

The Compounding Effect

Each generation of technology doesn't replace the last - it compounds with it.

  • You still have the tractor
  • You still have GPS and precision tools
  • Now you ADD unlimited intelligence to analyze and optimize

What could YOUR farm accomplish with the right AI tools? The potential is greater than any previous generation.

"AI will supercharge those trying to solve very complex problems"

The real power of AI isn't for simple tasks - it's for tackling complexity.

Who Benefits Most?

  • Problem solvers: Those already working on hard problems get multiplied capability
  • The curious: People who ask questions and dig deeper
  • The ambitious: Those tackling challenges they couldn't before

AI doesn't just help with the easy stuff - it opens doors to problems that were previously too complex to even attempt.

Complex Farm Problems AI Can Now Help Solve

  • Optimizing crop rotations across multiple fields, years, and markets
  • Balancing risk across insurance, marketing, and input decisions
  • Analyzing the true profitability of different enterprises
  • Understanding soil biology and microbiome interactions
  • Predicting equipment failures before they happen
  • Navigating complex government programs and compliance

The farms that tackle complexity - instead of avoiding it - will have the edge.

"Deflation" - A Skeptical Note

Zack mentioned deflation as a potential outcome of AI-driven efficiency...

The Farmer's Reality Check

Has this guy been in a grocery store lately?

While AI might drive down costs in some sectors, farmers see a different reality:

  • Input costs (fertilizer, seed, equipment) - UP
  • Labor costs - UP
  • Land costs - UP
  • Grocery prices consumers pay - UP
  • What farmers get paid - FLAT or DOWN

Take the "AI will make everything cheaper" promises with a grain of salt. The economics of farming don't always follow the tech world's predictions.

The Social Media Crisis - And a Solution

"The most addictive device and social media is insane. 4 times more likely to be depressed. We can fix it. One way: POLICY. Ban phones in schools."

The Problem

  • Smartphones and social media are designed to be addictive
  • Young people on social media are 4x more likely to be depressed
  • Constant connectivity disrupts focus, learning, and development
  • The technology optimizes for engagement, not wellbeing

The Fix: Policy Action

  • Ban phones in schools - a simple policy with proven results
  • Support local school boards implementing phone-free policies
  • Model healthy tech use at home
  • Create phone-free zones and times for your family

This is one area where policy - not just personal choice - can make a real difference.

"Reinvest in the Physical World"

Zack's call to action: As AI and digital tools expand, don't forget the real world.

What This Means

  • Physical skills still matter - mechanics, craftsmanship, hands-on work
  • Real relationships - face-to-face connections, community
  • Tangible assets - land, equipment, real property
  • Nature and outdoors - the antidote to screen time

Why This Matters for Farmers

Farming IS the physical world. You're already in the business everyone else needs to rediscover.

  • Your work with land, soil, crops, animals - this is real
  • Use AI to handle the digital/analytical work so you have MORE time for the physical
  • The farm is a great place to raise kids away from screens

"Digital abundance is not the end all." All the AI in the world doesn't grow food, repair equipment, or raise the next generation. The physical world remains essential.

"Even though the physical world is safer, the digital world is becoming more and more prevalent." The tension is real - we're being pulled into digital spaces even as we recognize the value of the physical. Awareness is the first step.

"Make a world that people want to be in and explore." - The ultimate goal of technology should be to enhance human flourishing, not replace human experience.

"Getting good at the internet is figuring out who is trying to trick you"

Critical insight from Zack on digital literacy in the AI age.

For Farmers Using AI

  • Not all AI tools are created equal - some are selling YOU

  • AI-generated content online may be misleading
  • Verify recommendations against trusted sources

"Text files are going to be the AI drivers"

A key technical insight about how AI will be controlled and customized.

What This Means

Simple text files - instructions you write in plain English - will tell AI systems what to do. No coding required.

  • System prompts: "You are my farm assistant. When I ask about prices, check CME first..."
  • Instruction files: Text documents that define how YOUR AI behaves
  • Custom rules: "Never recommend selling grain below $X. Always check basis at these 3 elevators..."
  • Your preferences: Written in plain language, not code

Why This Matters for Farmers

  • You don't need to be a programmer - just write clearly
  • YOUR knowledge and rules become the AI's guide
  • You can start building your "instruction library" now
  • The farmer who documents their decision rules has a head start

Action: Start writing down your farm's rules, preferences, and processes in simple text. These become your AI instructions.
